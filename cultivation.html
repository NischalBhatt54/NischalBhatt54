A look at the journey behind our cultivated Cordyceps militaris — from culture to fruiting body.

OUR APPROACH

Growing a delicate organism takes attention.

Cultivating Cordyceps militaris is not simply a matter of providing a substrate and waiting for a mushroom to appear.

Like many cultivated fungi, its development involves several stages, and each stage can involve its own set of environmental and biological considerations.

At SHILA, we approach cultivation as a controlled process in which cleanliness, observation, environmental management and consistency are given particular attention.

We deliberately work with limited production rather than trying to maximize volume at every stage. This allows us to devote greater attention to individual batches and the conditions in which they develop.

THE JOURNEY

From culture
to fruiting body.

The exact methods used by growers can differ depending on the strain, cultivation system, substrate and environmental conditions. What follows is a simplified overview of the general journey.

01

STARTING MATERIAL

Culture

Cultivation begins with a viable fungal culture. Depending on the cultivation system, this may be maintained through laboratory techniques such as agar or liquid culture.

Establishing the starting culture
02

PREPARATION

Substrate

A suitable nutritional substrate is prepared to provide the fungus with the resources needed for development.

Different growers may use different substrate compositions and preparation methods. Our specific formulation is part of our cultivation process and is not disclosed publicly.

Preparing the growing medium
03

CONTAMINATION CONTROL

Sterilization

Prepared substrate is generally treated to reduce or eliminate competing microorganisms before inoculation.

This stage is an important part of controlled fungal cultivation because unwanted microorganisms can interfere with fungal development.

Creating a cleaner starting environment
04

INOCULATION

Introducing the culture

The prepared substrate is introduced to the fungal culture under controlled, hygienic conditions.

From this point onward, the developing culture is monitored for signs of healthy colonization as well as anything that may indicate contamination or abnormal growth.

Giving the fungus a place to establish
05

DEVELOPMENT

Colonization

The fungal mycelium gradually develops through the substrate.

During this stage, growers generally pay attention to factors such as cleanliness, temperature, moisture and the condition of the developing culture.

Building the fungal network
06

FRUITING

Fruiting body development

Once the culture has developed sufficiently, conditions can be adjusted to encourage formation and development of the visible fruiting structures.

Light, temperature, humidity and airflow can all be relevant considerations during fruiting, although the appropriate conditions depend on the cultivation system and strain.

The stage where the familiar orange fruiting bodies emerge
07

HARVEST

Harvesting

When the cultivated fruiting bodies have reached the desired stage of development, they can be harvested from the substrate.

Harvest timing can depend on the strain, cultivation system and the characteristics the grower is aiming for.

Carefully separating the developed fruiting bodies
08

POST-HARVEST

Drying & preparation

Freshly harvested fruiting bodies contain substantial moisture and therefore require appropriate post-harvest handling.

Drying reduces moisture and helps prepare the cultivated material for storage and eventual use. The method and conditions used can influence the final product.

Preparing the harvest for its next stage

OUR PRIORITIES

What we focus on throughout the process.

01

Clean cultivation

Maintaining appropriate hygiene and contamination-control practices is a fundamental consideration when working with cultivated fungi.

02

Environmental control

We pay close attention to the cultivation environment and monitor conditions that can influence fungal development.

03

Observation

Cultivation is not entirely passive. Regular observation allows us to identify changes in the developing cultures and respond when necessary.

04

Small batches

We deliberately keep production limited. Smaller batches allow us to devote more attention to the cultivation process rather than pursuing volume alone.

BEHIND THE SCENES

Controlled, observed, adjusted.

Controlled cultivation gives us the ability to observe the crop throughout its development rather than relying entirely on natural environmental conditions.

Our cultivation environment is designed around the needs of the developing fungus. Temperature, humidity, lighting and airflow are among the factors that may be monitored or adjusted depending on the stage of growth.

The exact conditions used can vary between cultivation systems and strains, so we don't present a single set of numbers as a universal recipe for growing Cordyceps militaris.

✦

A NOTE ON OUR METHODS

We share the process, not the recipe.

We believe people should be able to understand where a cultivated product comes from and how it reaches its final form.

At the same time, every grower develops their own methods through experimentation, observation and experience. Some of the specific parameters and formulations we use are therefore kept as part of our cultivation know-how.

This page is intended to explain the journey, not provide a step-by-step production protocol.
